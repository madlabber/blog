I've been a fan of the Intel NUC as a virtualization platform for several generations of the product, and the current 8th generation of the platform in particular makes for a really good small form factor virtualization homelab platform. Tney are small, quiet, capable little lab boxes.

The i5 and i7 models have quad core hyperthreaded processors, up to 64GB of RAM, an M.2 slot that can take either an NVME drive or a SATA M.2 SSD, some versions have 2 M.2, others have an M.2 and a SATA.

Most models have single gigabit NIC onboard, and additional NICs can be added via thunderbolt 3 or USB3.

I chose to base my nodes on the NUC8i5BEH. This version has 4 cores, 8 threads, up to 64GB RAM, and support for both an M.2 NVME and a 2.5" SATA drive.

Here are the features in this model that are interesting from a virtualization standpoint:/  
Core i5, 4 Cores, 8 Threads  
Support for VT-X, VT-D, AES-NI  
Support for Intel Optane  
1 Thunderbolt 3 port  
4 external USB 3.1 ports  
  
For my travel lab build, I use just the onboard 1gb network port. For the homelab builds I use a second NIC, either USB3 or The apple TB NIC

William Lam has been able to verify several working 10GB adapters that can be connected to the TB3 port, and a thunderbolt expansion chassis could provide for dual 10GB and dual FC expansion, or additional storage. The thunderbolt 3 port really opens up the potential expandability for small form factor hosts, albeit at some significant investment.

Here I've added 2 USB3 network adapters, for a total of 4 NICs, consuming all of the rear ports on the NUC. In practice I only need 2 1GB ports, but I just wanted to see it work.  
  
Another interesting feature on the NUCs is iSCSI support. I want to see if I can iSCSI boot a NUC into Windows or VMware. With creative use of iSCSI boot, NUCs could be treated like the stateless hosts you would find in a blade style deployment.  
One feature lacking in the 8th generation NUCs is vPRO. This would have facilitated basic out of band management but for whatever reason intel chose not to include in on this generation of hardware.
